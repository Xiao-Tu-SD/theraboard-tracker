Project description

We are building a 12-hole interactive board for therapy. Holes in three shapes (circle, square, triangle) light up in a colour. The patient picks the matching piece and places it in the lit hole. A hidden magnet in each piece is sensed by a hall sensor under the hole, so the board knows when the piece is placed and how long it took.

The board gives voice feedback, shows the score on a small screen, and saves each session. A therapist can open a web page on a phone to see results over time.

Problem it solves

  • Normal shape sorters don't measure anything, so progress is judged by eye.
  • Patients find repetitive exercises boring and stop practising.
  • Therapists have no simple record of speed and accuracy across sessions.

Who it helps

  • Stroke survivors relearning hand control
  • Children with cerebral palsy, autism or ADHD
  • Older adults with memory problems
  • Occupational therapists and physiotherapists

Expected outcome (by 17 Nov 2026)

  • Working prototype: 12 holes that light up and detect pieces reliably.
  • Three therapy modes: colour match, reaction time, and memory sequence.
  • Feedback: voice prompts, score on the screen, and lights that turn green when correct.
  • Results page: a phone web page showing each session's time and accuracy.
  • Evidence: tested with 3–5 people, plus feedback from at least one doctor or therapist.
  • Documents: report, circuit diagram, demo video and presentation.
